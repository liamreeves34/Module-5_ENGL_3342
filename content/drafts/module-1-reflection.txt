For Module 1, I analyzed a mass email that the computer science department chair sent to every student and faculty member on the first day of early registration. Then I wrote my own professional email to Dr. Paudel, introducing myself and asking a question about the Module 2 assignment.
The hardest part was the sample itself. I had to redact every personal name without losing the course codes and UTRGV system names my analysis depended on, so I kept only what was already published in the course catalog and advising material. The chair’s email also made me rethink what “professional” means. It opened with “Hello Computer Science!”, defined none of its terms, and still worked, because every recipient already belonged to the department.
In my own email, the choice that worked best was stating my three purposes in the first paragraph and giving each one a heading, so Dr. Paudel could jump straight to the question. I also explained why I was asking: the answer would decide whether I removed all domain vocabulary or wrote for a reader fluent in a neighboring field.
