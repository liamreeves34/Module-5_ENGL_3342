Module 4 had three parts. In a Critical AI Literacy workshop, Tim Trasmonte and I compared Copilot’s responses to a vague prompt and a CLEAR-engineered prompt, then turned what we found into a one-page flyer for classmates, “Think Before You Prompt.” I also wrote a resume and cover letter for a bank analyst position, and a plain-language summary of my rail-heat research.
On the flyer, the challenge was keeping the evidence honest: both responses produced working code, and only the engineered one reasoned about its role. In the cover letter, I named my lack of banking experience in one sentence and pointed to my work with confidential data. In the summary, I had to explain why the study prices “slow orders” instead of the rare accidents they prevent.
The flyer works because it ends with action: a four-question checklist that includes “Did you disclose where you used AI?” In the job materials, specific numbers did the persuading. In the summary, defining each term where it first appeared kept a general reader with me.
