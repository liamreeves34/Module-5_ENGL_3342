Module 4 had two parts. In a Critical AI Literacy workshop, Tim Trasmonte and I compared Copilot’s responses to a vague prompt and a CLEAR-engineered prompt for the same coding task, then turned what we found into a one-page flyer for classmates, “Think Before You Prompt.” I also wrote a resume and cover letter for a business intelligence analyst position at a local bank.
The hardest part of the flyer was fitting honest evidence onto one page. On a fresh Copilot, both responses produced working code; only the engineered one reasoned about its role and goal, and that nuance had to fit in one line. In the cover letter, I had no banking experience, so I named that gap in one sentence and pointed to my work with confidential data.
What worked on the flyer was ending with action instead of warnings: four numbered messages, then a four-question checklist that includes “Did you disclose where you used AI?” In the job materials, specific numbers did the persuading, like the 338,460 casino sessions I analyzed and the 2,573 county-level results I checked against a separate implementation.
