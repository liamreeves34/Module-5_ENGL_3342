Module 4 consisted of three parts. During a Critical AI Literacy workshop, Tim Trasmonte and I compared Copilot’s answers to a vague prompt with its responses to a CLEAR-engineered prompt, then transformed our findings into a one-page flyer for classmates titled “Think Before You Prompt.” I also prepared a resume and cover letter for a bank analyst position, along with a plain-language summary of my rail-heat research.
The challenge with the flyer was presenting the evidence honestly: both responses generated working code, but only the engineered response considered its role. In the cover letter, I acknowledged my lack of banking experience in one sentence and highlighted my work with confidential data. For the summary, I needed to explain why the study prices “slow orders” rather than the rare accidents they are intended to prevent.
The flyer is effective because it concludes with a four-question action checklist, including “Did you disclose where you used AI?” In the job materials, specific numbers provided the persuasive force. In the summary, defining each term when it first appeared helped keep a general reader engaged.
